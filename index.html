Who can do what

7 roles · lowest rung first

Seven rungs, each the smallest set that lets somebody finish their own job. Nothing stops one person holding more than one role — that is a decision for whoever assigns them.

  1. HRMS_EMPLOYEE_SELF 1 permission

    The login an employee is handed for their own record. Reaches My HR and nothing else.

    Can

    • Their own payslips, attendance, leave balance and details, through /hrms/me

    Cannot

    • See any other employee, anywhere in the module
    • Apply for leave or submit a claim through the main screens — those endpoints are organisation-wide, so an employee holding them would see every colleague's leave and every colleague's claims. They are deliberately withheld.

    Watch out: The role is called HRMS_EMPLOYEE_SELF, not EMPLOYEE. A role named plainly EMPLOYEE collided with an existing one, and every workforce login issued silently received all 53 permissions instead of one.

  2. ATTENDANCE_TAKER 4 permissions

    Marks the daily attendance of the branch or office they are posted to.

    Can

    • See the employee list — the marking screen is a list of employees
    • View and mark attendance
    • See their own record, because whoever marks the roster is also on it

    Cannot

    • See what anybody is paid
    • Approve anything
  3. BRANCH_SUPERVISOR 16 permissions

    Runs a branch day to day: attendance, leave, expense claims and penalties for their own people.

    Can

    • Everything the attendance taker can
    • Approve leave
    • Raise a penalty
    • Pass an expense claim
    • Read departments, advances, contract workers and performance

    Cannot

    • See salary
    • Correct attendance on a day payroll has already consumed — that changes a figure somebody has been paid against, so it is an HR_ADMIN action
  4. HR_MANAGER 37 permissions

    Runs the module: employee master, wage master, statutory returns and payroll processing.

    Can

    • Create and edit employees
    • Manage departments, designations, grades and employment types
    • View salary and raise a pay revision
    • Process the monthly payroll run
    • Approve leave and expense claims, raise penalties and advances
    • Manage contract workers
    • Generate EPF, ESIC and Form-II returns

    Cannot

    • Approve a payroll run at either level — processing and approving must be two different acts, and this is the processing one
    • Sign the pay revision it raised, for the same reason
    • Delete an employee, or see unmasked identity numbers
  5. HR_ADMIN 46 permissions

    First signature: approves payroll runs and pay revisions at level 1, and corrects locked attendance.

    Can

    • Everything HR_MANAGER can
    • Approve a payroll run at L1, and a pay revision at L1
    • Override attendance on a locked day
    • Delete an employee record
    • See unmasked identity numbers (UAN, ESIC, PAN, Aadhaar)
    • Approve penalties, rate performance, read the payroll journal
    • Mark a released run paid, or cancel one

    Cannot

    • Release a run — that is the second signature, and it belongs to HR_HEAD
  6. HR_HEAD 27 permissions

    Second signature: releases payroll runs and pay revisions at level 2.

    Can

    • Approve a payroll run at L2, and a pay revision at L2 — the approvals that release either
    • Read the figures, the register behind them and the ledger they post to
    • Approve leave, expense claims and penalties
    • See unmasked identity numbers

    Cannot

    • Process payroll — this role cannot compute the run it releases
    • Create or edit employees, or touch the masters
  7. HEAD_OFFICE 23 permissions

    Head office and accounts: payroll settings, the wage master and the payroll ledger.

    Can

    • Manage payroll settings — PF and ESIC rates, professional tax, the duty base
    • Manage the wage master every employee's basic is looked up in
    • Manage designations, grades and employment types
    • Read widely across the module, because a rate cannot be set sensibly without seeing what it does

    Cannot

    • Sign at either level. Somebody who both sets the rates and releases the run should be given HR_HEAD as well, deliberately, rather than this role quietly including it.

SUPER_ADMIN and FLEET_ADMIN sit outside the ladder. The platform administrators, who already administer every other module. They hold the whole vocabulary including both signatures — somebody has to be able to unstick a tenant that has assigned its roles badly.

  • Two signatures, and they are different roles. A payroll run and a pay revision both need L1 then L2. That is why the top of the ladder is split: HR_MANAGER computes, HR_ADMIN signs first, HR_HEAD signs second. A run one person can compute, approve and release is not controlled at all.
  • But there is no different-person rule. Nothing stops one login holding HR_ADMIN and HR_HEAD and clearing both levels. The run records who signed each level, so the trail shows when they were the same person. If you want two people, assign two people.
  • A penalty needs only one approver. hrms:penalty:approve is the whole requirement. Do not wait for a second signature — the two-person rule belongs to payments in the Accounting module, not here.

EM-01

The workspace

0:41 /employee

Do this

  1. Sign in, then choose Employee Management on the module screen.
  2. Read the line under the title — that is your live headcount.
  3. Scan the four tiles: active employees, monthly wage bill, marked today, awaiting action.
  4. Use the Go to cards or the left sidebar to reach any screen in the module.

What the voiceover says

Welcome to Employee Management. Everything about your staff sits behind this one door. The line under the title shows eighty five active employees of eighty five on record. Across the top you get your monthly wage bill, how many people are marked today, and anything awaiting your action. Below that sits the latest payroll, September twenty twenty six, already paid, with gross, deductions, net paid, employer cost and C.T.C. The Go to cards, and the menu down the left, take you to every other part of the module.

EM-02

Departments, designations, grades

0:29 /employee/masters/departments

Do this

  1. Go to Masters, then Departments.
  2. Press New Department and fill name, code, description and order.
  3. Repeat on the Designations, Grades and Employment Types tabs — same pattern.
  4. Use Refresh if a row you just added has not appeared.

What the voiceover says

Before you add anybody, the system needs to know the shape of your organisation. Departments is the first master. Each row carries a name, a code, a description and a display order. Add one with New Department. Designations, Grades and Employment Types work exactly the same way, so once you have done this screen, you have done all four.

EM-03

Employee codes

0:37 /employee/masters/codes

Do this

  1. Go to Masters, then Employee Codes.
  2. Give each branch a short code; the table previews what codes there start with and the next code.
  3. Press Save Code Format.
  4. Do this before adding employees — a missing short code produces the old plain format instead.

What the voiceover says

Employee codes are built by the system, not typed by you. Each branch gets a short code, and the table shows you exactly what codes there will start with, and what the next code will be. A Bengaluru employee comes out as M.D.M dash B.L dash zero zero zero zero one. Set this up before you add people, and press Save Code Format when you are done. If a branch has no short code, the code quietly falls back to the older plain format.

EM-04

Basic master — wage rates

0:24 /employee/masters/basic

Do this

  1. Go to Masters, then Basic Master.
  2. Press New Rate and pick the designation and category.
  3. Enter basic per day, D.A. per day, the per-month figure and H.R.A.
  4. Check the figures — payroll computes straight off these rates.

What the voiceover says

Basic Master holds your base wage rates, one row per designation and category. You set basic per day, dearness allowance per day, the monthly figure and H.R.A. Payroll reads these rates directly, so a wrong number here becomes a wrong payslip for everybody on that designation. New Rate adds a row.

EM-05

Leave types

0:23 /employee/masters/leave-types

Do this

  1. Go to Masters, then Leave Types.
  2. Press New Type.
  3. Set name, code, annual quota, paid or unpaid, and carry forward.
  4. These are the only types that will appear when somebody applies for leave.

What the voiceover says

Leave Types decides what your staff can actually apply for. Each type has a name, a short code, an annual quota, whether it is paid, and whether the balance carries forward into next year. Casual Leave with a quota of twelve is a typical row. Add more with New Type.

EM-05B

Holidays

0:25 /employee/masters/holidays

Do this

  1. Go to Masters, then Holidays.
  2. Press New Holiday and enter the date, name and type.
  3. Load the whole year before the first payroll run.
  4. Mark national holidays correctly — N.H. overtime is paid at its own rate.

What the voiceover says

Holidays is the company calendar, the days everybody is off. Each entry has a date, a name and a type. Both the muster and payroll read this calendar, so load the full year before you run the first payroll. National holidays matter twice over here, because overtime worked on them is paid at a different rate.

EM-06

Payroll settings

0:36 /employee/salary-setup

Do this

  1. Open Payroll Settings from the sidebar.
  2. Work through the tabs: Earnings, Overtime, Provident Fund, ESIC, PT & LWF, Other.
  3. Set the organisation values first.
  4. For a branch that differs, change only the keys that differ — the rest keep falling back.
  5. Press Save settings.

What the voiceover says

Payroll Settings is where the statutory side lives, split across tabs: Earnings, Overtime, Provident Fund, E.S.I.C, P.T and L.W.F, and Other. The important thing to understand here is scope. You set these once for the organisation, and where a branch is different, you set only that one key on the branch. Everything you do not override keeps falling back to the organisation value. Press Save settings to apply.

EM-07

Face match

0:24 /employee/masters/face-match

Do this

  1. Go to Masters, then Face Match.
  2. Set the match threshold.
  3. Read the warning if the value is loose — a low threshold accepts the wrong face.
  4. Press Save Face Match Settings.

What the voiceover says

Face Match lets attendance be verified against a stored photograph. The threshold decides how close a match has to be before the system accepts it. Set it too loose and you will get false accepts, so the screen warns you when the value is risky. Save with Save Face Match Settings.

Add an employee

0:35 /employee/employees/new

Do this

  1. From Masters ▸ Employee Master press New Employee.
  2. Check the auto-filled employee code and the branch it reports detecting.
  3. Fill Identity, then Contact Information, then Employment & Role.
  4. Complete the statutory and document sections below.
  5. Press Create Employee.

What the voiceover says

Adding a person. The employee code is filled in for you, here M.D.M zero zero zero eight five, and the screen tells you which branch it detected from that prefix. Work down the sections in order: Identity, Contact Information, Employment and Role, and the statutory and document blocks below. Father's name is worth filling in, because it prints on the FORM two wage register. When everything is in, press Create Employee.

EM-10

One employee's record

0:33 /employee/employees/53

Do this

  1. Click any row in the employee list.
  2. Move through the tabs: Profile, Salary, Payslips, Leave, Attendance.
  3. Use Edit for ordinary corrections; Upload for documents.
  4. Issue a new password if the person has a login.

What the voiceover says

One person, with everything about them in five tabs: Profile, Salary, Payslips, Leave and Attendance. Along the top you can edit the record, deactivate or suspend the person, upload their documents, and issue a new password if they have a login. Pay is not edited casually here. A change to salary is raised as a revision, and then approved at two levels, L one and L two, before it takes effect.

EM-13

Marking attendance

1:03 /employee/attendance

Do this

  1. Open Attendance and set the date; narrow by department if you want.
  2. Check the tiles: on roster, saved, present, unmarked.
  3. Press Mark all present, then fix only the exceptions.
  4. Statuses: P present, A absent, HALF half day, WO weekly off, NH national holiday, OP off-day present, PL paid leave, UL unpaid leave.
  5. Enter overtime hours in the OT box on the row.
  6. With face check on, pick a status then photograph the person — 55% or better against their profile photo; you may mark through a failure.
  7. The Changes tab shows every edit, stamped with who made it.

What the voiceover says

Marking a day. Pick your date and, if you want, narrow to a department. The tiles tell you where you stand: on roster, saved, present, and still unmarked. Press Mark all present, then correct only the exceptions with the status buttons on each row. P for present, A for absent, HALF for a half day, W.O for a weekly off, N.H for a national holiday, O.P for off day present, and P.L or U.L for paid and unpaid leave. Overtime hours go in the box on the right. Where face checking is switched on, you pick a status and then photograph the employee. The face has to score fifty five per cent or better against their profile photo, though you may still mark through a failure. The Changes tab shows every edit, because each mark is stamped with who made it.

EM-14

The muster roll

0:30 /employee/muster

Do this

  1. Open Muster and choose the month.
  2. Scroll across to the totals: PRESENT DAY, OT Hours, NH OT, W.OFF, SUN.OT, ABSENT, TOTAL PAID.
  3. Reconcile anything that looks wrong before payroll, not after.
  4. Agree the sheet with the branch before the run is raised.

What the voiceover says

The Muster Roll is your whole month on one sheet. Scroll across, and the totals on the right are the numbers payroll actually pays on: present days, overtime hours, national holiday overtime, weekly offs, Sunday overtime, absent days and total paid days. This is the screen to check and agree before you run payroll, because after the run, these are the numbers everybody is paid against.

EM-15

Leave

0:25 /employee/leave

Do this

  1. Open Leave. Applications is the default tab.
  2. Review the row: employee, type, dates, days, reason.
  3. Approve or Reject from the row — give a reason when rejecting.
  4. Check the Balances tab to see the quota left.

What the voiceover says

Leave has two tabs, Applications and Balances. An application shows the employee, the type, the from and to dates, the number of days, a status and the reason given. Approve or reject from the row, and the employee's balance moves the moment you do. Apply for leave lets you file one on somebody's behalf.

EM-16

Penalties and advances

0:29 /employee/deductions

Do this

  1. Open Penalties & Advances.
  2. Press New Penalty; set type, amount and deduction month.
  3. Approve it — one approver is enough here.
  4. It shows as DEDUCTED once payroll picks it up.
  5. Advances live on the Advances tab: active while instalments run, then closed.

What the voiceover says

This is money recovered through the payslip, in two tabs. A penalty is raised with New Penalty: you choose the type, the amount, and the month it should be deducted in. It is then approved, and shows as deducted once payroll picks it up. Advances sit on the other tab. They stay active while the instalments run, and close when the money is fully recovered.

Expense claims

0:32 /employee/expenses

Do this

  1. Open Expenses.
  2. Press New Claim, add lines and attach the bill.
  3. Submit it for approval.
  4. The approver may approve a lower figure than claimed.

What the voiceover says

Expense claims move through draft, submitted, approved and paid. Each claim carries a number, the employee, a date, a title, the total claimed, the amount actually approved, and what has been paid. An approver may pass a smaller figure than the one claimed, and the bill stays attached to the claim. New Claim starts one, and the Categories tab controls what people are allowed to claim under.

EM-19B

Inside a payroll run

0:39 /employee/payroll/4

Do this

  1. Open a run from the register.
  2. Check the Sheet: attendance, earnings, deductions, employer contributions, computed.
  3. Approve at L1, then at L2 — both are required.
  4. Hand over from the tabs: Payslips, FORM-II, EPF, ESIC, Export Excel.
  5. Audit shows who did what on the run.

What the voiceover says

Inside a run you get the full sheet: every employee with their attendance, earnings, deductions, employer contributions and the computed result, column by column, from paid days and overtime hours through basic, dearness allowance, H.R.A, conveyance and mobile allowance. The tabs along the top are what you actually hand over: Payslips, FORM two, E.P.F, E.S.I.C, an audit trail, and Export Excel. A run is approved at two levels before the payslips are final.

EM-22

My HR — what the employee sees

0:34 /employee/me

Do this

  1. An employee with a login lands on My HR.
  2. They see only their own Payslips, Attendance, Leave and My details.
  3. They can change their own password.
  4. No other employee's data, no payroll and no settings are reachable from here.

What the voiceover says

Finally, this is what an ordinary employee sees when they sign in. My H.R gives them their own payslips, their own attendance, their own leave and their own details, and nothing else. No other employee, no payroll, no settings. They can change their own password here too. It is worth showing your staff this screen, because most of the questions they would otherwise bring to H.R are already answered on it.

Things that will trip you up

  • Payroll runs and pay revisions need two approvals. L1 then L2. There is no different-person rule, so the same login can clear both — but both must happen before payslips are final.
  • A penalty needs only one approver. Do not wait for a second signature. The two-person rule belongs to payments in the Accounting module, not here.
  • Payroll settings fall back key by key. A branch value overrides the organisation for that one key and nothing else. Set the organisation first, then override only what genuinely differs.
  • Set the employee-code prefixes before you add anybody. The code is built from the organisation prefix plus the branch short code. Miss a segment and it silently reverts to the old plain format.
  • Check the muster before the payroll run, not after. The totals on the right of the Muster Roll are what everybody is paid against.
  • An employee login reaches My HR and nothing else. Their own payslips, attendance, leave and details. No other employee, no payroll, no settings.
